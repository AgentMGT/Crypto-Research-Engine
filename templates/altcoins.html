What the scanner checks, and what you check

Checked automatically

  • Small, early cap: market cap under {{ alt.cfg.small_cap_usd|money }}.
  • Low liquidity: under {{ alt.cfg.low_liquidity_usd|money }} traded a day, but at least {{ alt.cfg.min_volume_usd|money }} so you can still get in and out.
  • Real-world use: categories such as DePIN, AI, real-world assets, payments, storage, compute, oracles or infrastructure.
  • Live products: money locked in its protocol on DefiLlama, recent GitHub commits, or a live product in its description.
  • Powers a platform, staking, server access, partnerships: read from the project's own description, so treat these as leads to verify.
  • Scarcity: a capped supply that is mostly in circulation, or a fully diluted value close to market cap (few unlocks ahead).
  • Active ecosystem, limited volume: at least two signs of a busy ecosystem (money locked in its protocol, 10+ GitHub commits in 4 weeks, 5,000+ CoinGecko watchlists, live on several chains, or X, Telegram and Discord all listed) while under 3% of its market cap trades a day. These are cards tagged "Quiet ecosystem".
  • Community: whether it lists X, Telegram and Discord, and how many CoinGecko users watch it.
  • News: headlines that name it on CoinGape, Changelly and Google News.

You check (tick "My checks" on each card; saved in this browser)

  • Open its Telegram, Discord and X: are people talking about the product, or only the price? Are the team posting updates?
  • Confirm partnerships on the partner's own site or account, not just the project's.
  • Try the product, check the staking page, look at who holds the supply on the explorer.

✓ = the data supports it, ✗ = the data points the other way, ? = can't tell from free data. Telegram, Discord and X can't be read without paid access, so the scanner links to them instead.
